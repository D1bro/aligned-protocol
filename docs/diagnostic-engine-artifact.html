Aligned · Internal Spec

Diagnostic Engine

Content and schema for turning a low score into a diagnosed, specific next action — instead of ten separate mini‑systems, one model, pressure‑tested against three deliberately different areas.

Status: design accepted Schema: not yet implemented Areas tested: 3 of 10 Bands tested: Stabilise only
01 — Why this exists

One model, not ten

Every life area needs the same underlying capability: turn a low score into something more useful than a generic tip. Rather than hand‑author ten separate systems, the idea was pressure‑tested against three deliberately different areas — one that reduces to arithmetic (Money), one driven by external situations (Relationships), and one internal and self‑reported (Confidence) — until a single model held for all three. This document is that model.

“Aligned should not rush to explain the user to themselves. It should help the user gather enough information to understand themselves more accurately.”

02 — The engine loop

Twelve steps, one cycle

The score decides where to look; everything after it exists to earn the right to prescribe anything.

1
ScoreThe client's satisfaction rating for the area, out of 10.
2
PriorityImportance × distance from full alignment decides which area becomes the focus.
3
BandThe score sorts into one of five severity bands — decides what kind of help is appropriate.
4
Pattern sentenceA non‑judgemental, non‑assuming description of what this band and area typically feels like.
5
DiagnosticA short structured "Reality Check" that starts finding out what's actually going on.
6
Reported patternWhat the client says is happening — a hypothesis, not a fact.
7
Clarifying questions / observationA second pass — sometimes immediate, sometimes logged over days — that tests the reported pattern.
8
Working interpretationAligned's current best read, carrying a confidence level, not a verdict.
9
Appropriate actionThe specific next thing — chosen by the interpretation, not the raw score.
10
BehaviourThe client actually does it.
11
ReviewWhat happened, what worked, what didn't.
12
Update interpretationThe working read gets sharper for next time. The loop continues.
03 — Severity bands

What kind of help, not just how much

Only Stabilise has been designed in depth so far. The postures below Establish are a working hypothesis, not yet pressure‑tested the way Stabilise was.

BandScorePosture
Stabilise1–3Diagnose before prescribing. Establish facts and context before recommending any change.
Establish4–5Build a basic structure or habit where essentially none exists yet.
Improve6–7Sharpen and add consistency to a foundation that's already there.
Optimise8–9Refine, add nuance, remove friction.
Maintain / Expand10Protect what's working; look outward from this area to others.
04 — Interpretation model

The client's answer is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis

The single most important finding from the Confidence pressure test. Every working interpretation also carries a diagnostic certainty — low, medium, or high. One multiple‑choice answer produces a low‑certainty hypothesis; an observation task played out over days can raise it. Never treat one answer as a permanent classification of who someone is.

Reported pattern

What the client says is happening — their answer to the first diagnostic question.

Working hypothesis

What Aligned provisionally thinks may be causing it. Never shown as settled fact.

Observed evidence

What later questions, or logged behaviour, actually support or contradict.

Intervention route

What Aligned recommends next, given all three of the above.

05 — Branching

Two layers before anything gets prescribed

Layer 1 · Initial classification

Score + band + first question

Sorts the client into a broad reported pattern.

“Context‑dependent confidence” — solid in some areas, not in others.
↓ tests the pattern before acting on it
Layer 2 · Cause clarification

A further question, or an observation task

Produces something specific and actionable — or, sometimes, another diagnostic rather than an action yet.

“Confidence holds up when prepared and experienced, drops in unfamiliar situations — likely an exposure/practice gap, not a general self‑belief problem.”
06 — Action types

Not every next step is a checklist

TypeAsks of the clientExample
Immediate diagnosticAnswer a short structured set, onceMoney Reality Check
Structured reflectionAnswer open reflection questionsRelationship Reality Check
Behavioural actionDo one defined thingHave one intentional conversation
Observation taskNotice and log over a set windowNotice self‑doubt triggers, 7 days
Repeated practiceDo a small thing several timesThree graduated exposure actions
Tracking taskLog a simple measure dailyRecord sleep for 7 days
Decision taskChoose between named optionsChoose your primary financial priority
ReviewLook back and re‑interpretReview what changed after 30 days

An observation task doesn't resolve immediately — what it resolves to depends on what gets logged:

duration_days:     7
prompt:            "Notice when self-doubt appears."
capture_fields:    [situation, thought, feeling, behaviour, what_happened_next]
min_observations:  3
completion_rule:   duration elapsed OR minimum observations reached
resolution:        analyse logged observations, assign the next diagnostic branch
07 — Worked examples

Stabilise band, condensed

Money & Stability

2 / 10 · diagnosed by arithmetic

First action: a 30‑Day Money Reality Check — available cash, confirmed 30‑day income, essential 30‑day outgoings, resulting surplus or shortfall. The result classifies directly: surplus → organise it; small shortfall → close the gap; serious shortfall → protect essentials and create income; debt/arrears → protect essentials first. A rare case where Layer 2 is arithmetic rather than another question.

Relationships & Connection

2 / 10 · diagnosed by situation

First question sorts into a reported pattern — not enough connection, connection without depth, one difficult relationship, withdrawal, repeating unhealthy patterns, neglect through lack of time, unclear what's wanted. For "one difficult relationship," Layer 2 is itself still diagnostic — what's happening, what's needed, has it been said, what's being tolerated — before resolving into conversation, boundary, distance, or reconsideration.

Confidence & Self‑Belief

2 / 10 · diagnosed by pattern

The reported pattern here is the client's own theory about themselves, and may be incomplete. The context‑dependent branch matters most: Layer 2 asks what actually changes between confident and unconfident situations — experience, preparation, familiarity, who's watching, stakes, expected judgement, prior success — which can separate a real self‑belief problem from an exposure‑and‑practice gap wearing the same 2/10.

08 — Schema

Proposed tables

Follows the existing migration's conventions — uuid keys via gen_random_uuid(), text fields with check (... in (...)) in place of enum types, timestamptz audit columns. Nine new tables plus one column added to goals. Full DDL lives in docs/diagnostic-engine.md alongside this page — this is the shape, not the file.

TableHolds
bandsContent table for the five severity bands — label and posture text, editable without a redeploy.
interventionsOne row per life area + band: the entry diagnostic experience.
intervention_questionsOrdered questions per intervention; can depend on a prior answer (Layer 2 only appearing for one reported pattern).
intervention_question_optionsMultiple‑choice options for select‑type questions.
actionsThe recommend‑next content — title, purpose, type, and a config jsonb shaped by that type.
diagnostic_outcomesMaps one answer to what happens next — another intervention, or an action.
client_diagnostic_responsesWhat a specific client actually answered, tied to their CLEAR plan.
client_observationsOne row per logged occurrence for an observation‑type action.
client_action_progressA client's live instance of a resolved action, carrying the working hypothesis and its confidence level.
goals (altered)Gains resolved_action_id — the library seeds the default, free‑text fields stay for personalising or overriding it.
09 — Open decisions

Before any of this gets built

1

Suggestion vs. auto‑assign

Does a resolved action become the client's goal automatically, or a suggestion the client — or Duane, as coach — can accept, edit, or replace? Leaning toward suggestion‑with‑override, since CLEAR's premise is the client arriving at their own goal. A product call, not an engineering one.

2

Content authoring for v1

Hand‑author rows for the three designed areas to get something live quickly, or build a small content‑editor UI first? Faster to ship versus easier to review and extend later.

3

How dynamic is the pattern sentence?

Fixed per band right now. Once Layer 2 resolves, should client‑facing copy update to reflect the specific finding, reusing working_hypothesis as display text rather than just internal state?
